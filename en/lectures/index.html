Lectures and a knowledge archive

Lectures are not tedious material: they are a form of imagined questions and answers, collected as lectures for convenience. Each has a topic and a format for an extended conversation with the listener. You can ask questions to explore the material more deeply and in the direction you need.

The lectures are a concentrated archive of what we gradually explore across our publications, brought into a single form for anyone who wants to examine the underlying causes in a convenient way.

L↗

No preparation is required. You do not need to be a scientist, know mathematics, or finish something else to earn the next step. Open a lecture whose title catches your attention. If it works for you, continue. If it does not, close it and choose another. There is no course to pass and no falling behind. Read for recognition: where have you seen this before? A lecture about experience may recall something you learned; one about price may recall what you paid for; one about a channel may reveal where habit carries you. A text becomes yours when you recognize your own life in it.

Disagree. Return and mark where you were unconvinced. You are a participant in a conversation, not a pupil taking a test. We are interested in your objection, which moves research further than a polite nod. Try one thought in practice: ask a new question, look differently at a familiar situation, or take a step you have postponed. From that moment, you are conducting research rather than merely reading about it.

How to work with the lectures?

You can begin with any lecture. Their titles express their structure and their directions: some address the universe and its general structural questions, while others turn towards human experience, the soul, love, creativity and responsibility.

Reading a lecture is itself a cycle. You do more than receive information: the questions you can ask begin to change. You can return to the same lecture a year later and read another text because you have changed. If you notice a contradiction, a weak point or a stronger formulation, that is an invitation to work with the material. The archive is open. Ask questions.

Read as you wish. Simply read.
